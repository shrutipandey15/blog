Finished it at 1am on a work night, which was stupid, and then thought about it through two standups and a code review I did not read.

He's awful. Three hundred pages of awful. Then on page three hundred and one he notices she's cold, gives her his coat, and I shut the book and thought — out loud, in my own kitchen, like a person — he's been through so much.

He has been through NOTHING. He is ink. I had a worse Tuesday than he had an entire fictional life. Nobody gave me a coat. I got feedback.

I think we practise on them. All that understanding nobody is asking us for, all that patience we're handing out for free at work and on the phone to our mothers — we take it somewhere it actually pays.

> And it does pay, in there. You find the wound under the awful, you hand over the grace, and within forty pages he changes.

He was always going to. Somebody decided that in a Word document in 2019.

Real people don't have a Word document. That's the whole thing. There's no page three hundred and one scheduled for anyone I actually know, and I keep checking.

Anyway. I'll do it again on Thursday.
