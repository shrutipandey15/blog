Kamala had never returned a book late. Not once in thirty-one years. Not through her mother's illness. Not through the flood in '04, when the ground floor took four inches of water and she waded in with a canvas bag over her head.

The book was nothing. A biography of a minor Mughal administrator, spine gone, somebody's chai ring on page ninety. Due Thursday.

On Thursday she put it in her bag. On Thursday she took it out again.

> She wanted to know what sort of woman she'd be by Friday. It was the only experiment available to her and it cost eight rupees a day.

By Sunday the fine was thirty-two rupees and she had read the thing twice, which she had also not done in thirty-one years, because a book you must return on Thursday is a book you read at Thursday's speed.

She took it back on the fourteenth day and paid in coins, slowly, giving the girl at the desk every opportunity. The girl did not look up. Kamala understood then — and it embarrassed her later, on the bus — that nobody had ever been watching.
