```verse
No one asks,
“How are you?”
“Have you eaten?”
“Did you get something for yourself?”
The questions are always different.
“Can you do this?”
“Can you pay for that?”
“When will you get this done?”
And if I do, the answer is still the same.
“What are you even doing for us?”
```

```verse
I stopped buying things I needed.
Slipper?
“Kaam toh chal hi jaa raha hai.”
The books I quietly put back.
The dress that stayed on the hanger.
The little dreams I kept postponing until they forgot my name.
They could wait. Everyone else couldn't.
```

```verse
Then one day, my medicine cost three hundred rupees.
Three hundred.
Cash on delivery.
I stood counting notes before opening the door.
The same hands that didn't hesitate to spend thirty thousand on an air conditioner.
```

> That's when I understood. It was never about money. It was about whose need mattered.

```verse
And still
“What are you even doing for us?”
```

```verse
Then came another lesson.
Save.
Not to feel secure. Not to breathe easier.
Save for your wedding.
Because you're a strong, independent woman.
Strong enough to earn.
Strong enough to give.
Strong enough to never need anything back.
```

```verse
Until one day I finally say,
“I'm tired.”
“Please sometimes just call to ask me how am I doing????”
```

```verse
Suddenly,
I'm selfish. I'm disrespectful. I'm the villain.
My pain disappears, their pain begins.
My years of sacrifice are forgotten,
but my one moment of honesty becomes unforgettable.
```

> Maybe that's what hurts. Not that I kept giving. But that after everything my worth could still be questioned.

```verse
And after all these years,
after every sacrifice,
every postponed dream,
every swallowed need,
I'm still left with the same sentence echoing in my ears
“What are you even doing for us?”
```
