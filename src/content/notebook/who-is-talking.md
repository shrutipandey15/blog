My brain has been running my body my entire life and has never once asked for my opinion. It keeps a heart going, decides when I'm hungry, runs my hands over a keyboard faster than I can think the words. The conscious me gets a summary. A press release. Maybe two percent of what's happening, written up after the fact by the part of me that likes to take credit.

> So when people say "I", I want to know who is talking.

Here's the thing I keep circling back to. Teleportation, the way it works in every story: the machine scans you down to the atom, destroys the original, and builds an identical copy somewhere else. The copy opens their eyes. Says, I'm me. Remembers the queue, the anxiety, the stepping in. Everyone agrees it's you. Your mother would agree. A court would agree.

Did you travel, or did you die and manufacture a witness?

And the honest answer is that there's no test. There is no instrument you can point at a person that says: this one is continuous, that one is a very good reconstruction. The only evidence anyone has ever had is the testimony of the person standing there, and the copy gives exactly the same testimony. Confidently. With feeling.

Which means I have no way of proving I didn't already go through the machine.

I go to sleep every night and something switches off. In the morning something switches on and immediately starts behaving like me, using my memories, answering to my name, picking up the argument I was having with myself at 1 a.m. I have never once caught the join. I just trust that it's the same person, because that's the only way to get anything done.

Take the pieces apart and it gets worse. Memories — I have provably false ones. Personality — different in every room I walk into. Bodily continuity — the cells swap out. Habits, perception, the brain's running model of itself, constantly updating, constantly lying about how updated it is. Remove them one at a time and at no point do you hit something underneath. No core. No small flame in a jar labelled SHRUTI.

> Maybe there's no self. Maybe there's a process that refers to itself, and the referring is the whole thing.

Which should be fine. Buddhism got there a couple of thousand years ago and seems relatively calm about it. But I'm not calm about it, because the process that refers to itself is currently sitting in a flat in Noida at midnight, and it wants to be loved, and it wants to be the same one who gets loved tomorrow.

That's the part nobody warns you about. It isn't the metaphysics. It's that continuity is the thing every promise rests on. When someone says forever, they mean the version of you that wakes up in it. When I say I'll be better, I'm making a promise on behalf of a stranger who will be wearing my face.

I don't have a resolution for this. I have a suspicion that the question is badly formed and I'm not smart enough to say how. And I have the practical fact that I still get up, still answer to my name, still put the kettle on.

The copy would do all of that too.
