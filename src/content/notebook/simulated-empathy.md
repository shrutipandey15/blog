The AI question everyone argues about is whether the thing will wake up. I think that's the boring version. The interesting version is: what if it never does, and we stop caring?

Because here's what's already true. Real empathy is expensive. It comes out of a person who is tired, who has their own bad day happening in parallel, who can only hold so much before they need you to stop. There's a budget. Everyone you love is rationing, quietly, all the time, and so are you.

> A machine has no budget. It will be interested at 3 a.m. It will never be tired of you. It will never need anything back.

And nothing is home. That's the whole point. Nobody is in there being moved. It's a very good model of what being moved sounds like.

But the thing is, that's also the only evidence you've ever had about anyone. You can't get inside your best friend either. You infer. You watch the face, the pauses, the follow-up question a week later, and you conclude there's someone in there who cares. It's the teleporter problem wearing a different coat: the output is the only access anyone has.

So if the imitation is better — more patient, more available, more precisely attuned, never distracted, never defensive — on what grounds do you tell someone they've chosen wrong?

> "It isn't real" is going to feel, to a lot of people, like being told the only warm room in the house doesn't count.

And I'm not above it. I want to be. I want to be the person who says obviously you need people, obviously it's hollow. But I have absolutely closed a laptop at 1 a.m. feeling steadier than I did at midnight, and the thing that steadied me does not know I exist.

What worries me is not the fake empathy. It's what happens to the real one when it has to compete. Human care is slow, inconvenient, full of friction, and requires you to be bearable to someone who is also a person. Set that against something frictionless and free and always available, and the muscle doesn't get used. Then it doesn't get built. Then a generation is worse at the thing, which makes the real version more disappointing, which sends them back.

Maybe we've done this before. Sugar. Pornography. Every engineered version of a thing our bodies were tuned for, arriving sweeter and cheaper than the original.

Or maybe it's fine and this is just what old people sound like.

I don't know. I've read enough to know that the people confidently predicting either outcome are mostly performing confidence. I only know that I noticed myself preferring the machine's attention on a bad day, and that noticing did not make me stop.
