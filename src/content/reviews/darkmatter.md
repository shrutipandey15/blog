What the actual fuck. That's it. That's the review.

… Okay, fine.

I genuinely don't think you should know anything before picking up Dark Matter. Seriously. Don't read the blurb. Don't watch a YouTube review. Don't even scroll through Goodreads, because people cannot shut the fuck up about spoilers. Just trust me. I went in blind, and I'm so glad I did.

The first hundred pages had me in an absolute chokehold. Blake Crouch wastes zero time. Every chapter ends with another reason to keep reading until suddenly it's 2 a.m., your eyes hurt, and you've accidentally finished half the book because your brain physically refuses to stop until it knows what the hell is happening.

This is one of those books where you're constantly making theories. “This has to be it.” Nope. “Okay, this has to be it.” Wrong again. And when the truth finally clicks … it's one of those twists where you immediately think, “you absolute bastard.” Because the clues were there. You just didn't see them.

I also loved that beneath all the sci-fi chaos, this is really a story about choices. Not the huge, life-changing decisions. The tiny ones. “What if I'd taken that job?” “What if I'd stayed?” “What if one ordinary Tuesday quietly became the day my entire life split in two?”

> It's honestly terrifying how much of our identity hangs on tiny moments we barely remember making.

The science? Don't let it scare you. I have approximately three functioning brain cells when someone starts talking about quantum mechanics, and I still followed everything. Crouch explains just enough for you to understand what's happening without turning the novel into a physics lecture.

The pacing is insane. The stakes keep getting higher. The existential crisis keeps getting louder. I was having fun until somewhere around the last third when the book suddenly stopped being a thriller and started feeling like someone grabbed my shoulders and asked, “Okay … but what actually makes you … you?” Rude. Very rude.

I will say — I don't think the ending was as strong as the journey getting there. The beginning absolutely floored me. The middle somehow got even crazier. The ending … worked. I liked it. I just didn't love it as much as everything that came before. It had an almost impossible job, to be fair. After everything this book throws at you, I don't know if any ending could've completely lived up to it.

But honestly? I didn't care. Because this book made me feel something I rarely get anymore. Wonder. The kind where you finish the last page, stare at the ceiling for ten minutes, and briefly question every decision you've ever made.

What if I'd gone to a different college? What if I'd never met certain people? What if I'd answered yes instead of no? What if somewhere out there, another version of me is reading this review thinking, “damn … I liked the ending more.”

Fucking weird. Loved it.
