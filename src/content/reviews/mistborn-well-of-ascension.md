I have a feeling Brandon Sanderson has figured out a very specific way to torture his readers.

Give them an incredible first book, make them fall in love with the characters, then make the second book slightly less good while quietly planting a hundred things they won't understand until the last few chapters.

Because The Well of Ascension was good. I enjoyed it. But it was not as good as The Final Empire.

And I think the biggest reason is that the story just didn't have the same momentum.

The first book had a very clear goal: overthrow the Lord Ruler.

This one starts after they've actually done it, which sounds like it should be just as exciting. Except now the Lord Ruler is dead, Luthadel is surrounded by armies, Elend is trying to figure out how to rule, different factions are fighting for power, and everyone is trying to keep a newly liberated world from immediately collapsing into chaos.

> There is something I really like about that premise. The revolution is over, and now comes the much harder question: what do you do with the freedom you fought for?

But the execution felt slower to me.

There was a LOT of politics, planning, conversations and people trying to decide what should happen next. And while I understood why all of it mattered, I didn't always find it interesting.

Then there was Vin.

I still love Vin, but this book puts her in a very different kind of conflict. In the first book, she was learning that she could be more than the girl who had spent her entire life surviving. Here, she has become incredibly powerful and is somehow even more uncertain about herself.

She can fight. She can kill. She can use Allomancy better than almost anyone.

But apparently none of that helps when you start wondering whether you're actually good enough for someone.

The Vin and Elend situation drove me a little insane.

I understood Vin's insecurities. I understood why she doubted herself and why she thought Elend deserved someone better. I just didn't feel the relationship enough to care about all the back-and-forth. At some point I wanted to tell them both to sit down and have one normal conversation.

Elend himself was more interesting to me this time, though. Watching him try to become a ruler instead of simply being the bookish guy who happened to become king was one of the more interesting parts of the political storyline.

And then there is Straff.

What an absolute bitch of a father.

I don't think I've ever read a character where my reaction was so consistently, oh, I fucking hate this man.

Which means, of course, that he was effective.

Zane was another character I wasn't expecting, and his presence added a completely different kind of tension to Vin's story. He made her question herself even more, and there was something unsettling about the way he understood parts of her that she didn't really understand herself.

But my favourite surprise was OreSeur.

I loved him.

And I felt terrible for him at the same time.

The whole kandra situation was fascinating, but what really got me was his relationship with Vin and the fact that he eventually chose to break his contract for her—even knowing that doing so was an executable offence.

That meant something.

It wasn't just another magical creature doing its assigned job. There was loyalty there, and choice, and consequence. And somehow this very strange, occasionally terrifying creature ended up being one of the characters I cared about most.

Sazed, obviously, remains lovely.

But his storyline also made the book feel much heavier. He's dealing with questions of faith, purpose and what he actually believes, and I really liked seeing that side of him. He's one of those characters who seems calm and dependable until you realise there is an entire storm happening underneath.

And I still really like the crew. One of my favourite things about this series is that they don't feel like generic fantasy side characters. They all have their own quirks and skills, and you understand why Kelsier chose them in the first place.

Although… I missed Kelsier. A lot.

The first book had this ridiculous energy because Kelsier was constantly driving everything forward. His absence is felt here—not just because he's gone, but because the people he left behind are now trying to figure out how to continue without him.

And that's probably one of the strongest things this book does. It makes you live with the consequences of the first book.

The world itself also gets much bigger. We learn more about the Well, the Deepness, the kandra, the Lord Ruler and the history behind the things we thought we understood.

And Sanderson's foreshadowing is still ridiculous.

There were moments where I thought I knew where the story was going. I absolutely did not.

He'll spend hundreds of pages giving you information that seems completely unrelated, and then suddenly something happens and you're sitting there thinking: OH. So that mattered.

The ending is where this book won me back.

Because the last part is once again Sanderson basically emptying an entire cupboard of secrets onto the table and expecting me to act surprised that he had been hiding them there the whole time.

And yes, I was surprised. The twists actually twist.

I still think The Final Empire had the stronger story and the stronger overall reading experience for me. The Well of Ascension had stretches where I was genuinely bored, and I don't think the political storyline was strong enough to carry that much of the book on its own.

But I also can't call it a bad book. Because by the end, the story had changed again.

> The Lord Ruler was never the whole problem. The rebellion was never the whole story.

And apparently Sanderson has decided that every time I think I've finally understood this world, he's going to move the goalpost.

Which is incredibly annoying. And, unfortunately for me, incredibly effective.

I'm going into The Hero of Ages with approximately zero trust in this man.

Let's see how that goes.
