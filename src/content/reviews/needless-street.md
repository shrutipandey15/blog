> What the actual fuck did I just read?

I finished The Last House on Needless Street and then just stared at the ceiling for a while.

Not because I was trying to understand the ending.

Because I was trying to process the fact that I had apparently been reading one story while the book was quietly telling me another one underneath it.

This is such a strange book to review because almost everything I want to say about it risks ruining the experience. And I really don't want to do that, because half the fun is being completely unsure of what the hell is going on.

We have Ted, living in his strange little house with his cat Olivia and his daughter Lauren. We have Dee, who has spent eleven years convinced that Ted is responsible for her little sister Lulu's disappearance and moves next door with one very specific purpose: to find out what happened to her.

And then there is Olivia.

A cat.

Who has her own perspective.

And somehow that is one of the most bizarrely convincing parts of the book.

Everyone in this story feels like they are carrying something you aren't being allowed to see yet. Ted's memories don't feel trustworthy. Dee is consumed by what happened to Lulu. Lauren is unsettling in ways that are difficult to explain without giving too much away. And Olivia is just... Olivia, trying to make sense of the strange human she has decided is hers to protect.

The further I got, the more uncomfortable I became.

Not necessarily because the book was frightening in the traditional horror sense, but because I couldn't trust my own understanding of what I was reading.

And I LOVE that in a thriller.

There were moments when I thought I'd figured it out.

I had not.

Not even remotely.

The book keeps giving you little pieces and letting you build your own explanation, only to quietly pull another piece out from underneath it. And the really impressive part is that when everything finally comes together, it doesn't feel like the author just pulled a ridiculous twist out of nowhere.

The clues were there.

I just didn't see them.

> OH HELL NO.

That's basically my reaction to the ending.

I don't think I've ever been happier to realise that I was completely wrong.

What I also didn't expect was how sad this book would make me. Beneath the mystery, the horror and all the things that made me deeply uncomfortable, there is so much about trauma, loneliness, memory, survival and the ways people try to protect themselves from things they cannot bear to face.

And somehow, despite how strange these characters are, I cared about them.

Even Ted.

Especially Ted.

Which is a sentence I absolutely did not expect to be writing when I started this book.

Catriona Ward has written something genuinely unsettling here—not because she constantly tries to scare you, but because she makes you question what you're seeing. The story keeps shifting beneath you until you don't know whether you should be afraid of the house, the people inside it, your own assumptions, or all three.

I went in expecting a creepy mystery.

I got something much stranger, much darker and much sadder.

And now I'm sitting here staring at my ceiling wondering how I didn't see it coming.

> I hate it when books do that. I love it when books do that.
