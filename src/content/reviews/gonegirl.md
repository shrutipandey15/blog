> Okay. I finally get it.

I finally understand why people are obsessed with Gone Girl and the whole unreliable narrator thing, because apparently I just needed this specific book to understand what everyone has been talking about for years.

Genuinely, I never got it. Every unreliable narrator I had read before was some drunk, paranoid, deeply irritating woman making me want to pull my hair out while screaming why does nobody believe me. I sat there wondering why anyone enjoyed this.

And then Amy Dunne happened.

Oh. This is what you meant. Because Amy is terrifying, but she is also smart. She's calculating. She's completely unhinged, yes, and she knows exactly what she is doing at every moment. That's the difference. Suddenly I understood the appeal.

The best part is that Nick and Amy are both horrible people and both completely fascinating. I kept reading purely to find out what these two were going to do to each other next.

And the way the story keeps letting you think you've worked it out before going actually, no. That was so much fun. Even when I had a rough idea of where things were heading, I still wanted to watch Flynn get there.

Gillian Flynn is also mean with her writing and I love it. So many observations about marriage, expectations, the versions of ourselves we perform for other people. Underneath all the completely insane things happening, there's a lot of uncomfortable truth.

That said, the second half lost me a little. Some of the character decisions started feeling like okay Gillian, you're doing this because the plot needs you to. And the ending left me sitting there like, that's it? After all that?

Still. I had so much fun. I hated these people. I was fascinated by these people. I wanted to throw the book across the room and immediately pick it back up.

Do I think it's perfect? No. Do I finally understand the unreliable narrator obsession? Unfortunately, yes.

> Amy Dunne, you horrible, brilliant, absolutely insane woman. I get it now.
