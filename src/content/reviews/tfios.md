> I hate this book. Absolutely hate it.

I hate that it made me laugh so much. I hate that it made me smile so much. I hate that I fell completely in love with these characters only for this book to turn around and destroy me.

I hate that I knew I was going to cry and somehow still wasn't prepared for how much.

I read this in my first year of college and I remember finishing it and just sitting there. Thinking about the book. Thinking about the lines. Thinking about Hazel and Augustus. Thinking about how unfairly beautiful the whole thing was.

And I hate that I still remember so many of those lines. Years later. Without trying.

> Because that's the thing about pain. It demands to be felt.

WHY. Why would you do that to me, John Green. And then there's okay. Just okay. I don't think I have ever hated a word so much.

Because somewhere along the way Hazel and Augustus stopped being characters I was reading about and became people I was ridiculously attached to. Their stupid jokes. Their sarcasm. Augustus and his metaphors. Hazel deciding she's a grenade. The way they fall in love knowing exactly how temporary all of it is.

And Augustus Waters. I hate that I fell in love with this boy. The cigarette between his teeth that he never lights because he refuses to give it the power to kill him. The ridiculous confidence. The slightly crooked smile. The way he makes Hazel want to live past the life she had already quietly accepted for herself.

That's the trick of this book, and it is a trick. It isn't just sad. It makes you laugh first. It makes you fall in love first. It hands you all these warm, funny, small, ordinary moments and then reminds you that none of them were ever guaranteed to last.

Maybe that's why it hurt as much as it did. Hazel thinks loving people means eventually destroying them. Augustus wants to leave a mark on the world. They both know exactly what is coming and they choose each other anyway.

I stayed up reading this. I cried. I finished it and cried more. And then I couldn't sleep because my head was full of lines and feelings I couldn't put anywhere.

> My thoughts are stars I cannot fathom into constellations.

Which is, annoyingly, the only accurate way to describe what that night was like.

So yes. I hate this book. I hate how much it made me laugh. I hate how much it made me love. I hate how much it hurt. I hate that years later I still think about it. And I hate that every single time I think about it, I know exactly what I actually mean when I say all of this.

I love this book. I really, really do. And I'm still grateful for that little infinity.
