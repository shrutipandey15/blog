Janice Hallett is becoming that author for me. The kind where I don't even need to know what the book is about anymore. Just tell me she wrote it, and I'm in.

I will say though — this one took a minute to win me over. The first 20–25% felt like I'd accidentally joined someone's university Teams channel. There were assignments, discussions about multimedia art, project briefs, tutor feedback, more assignments … and as someone with precisely zero knowledge or talent in art, there were definitely moments where my eyes glazed over a little. I wasn't bored exactly, I just wanted someone to disappear already.

Thankfully, Janice Hallett knows exactly what she's doing. Because once things started moving … they really started moving. One thing I love about her books is that she never treats the reader like a passive observer. You're investigating too. Every email, every group chat, every diary entry, every passive-aggressive message feels like evidence, and you're convinced you've cracked it.

Reader, you haven't.

I had theories. So many theories. Every single time I thought I'd figured out who was lying, someone else would do something suspicious and I'd throw my entire investigation out the window.

And then that twist. I genuinely love twists that don't come out of nowhere. I don't want an author to pull a rabbit out of a hat in the last chapter — I want to feel slightly stupid because the clues were sitting in front of me the entire time.

> I don't want an author to pull a rabbit out of a hat in the last chapter — I want to feel slightly stupid because the clues were sitting in front of me the entire time.

Looking back, they're everywhere. Tiny details that seem meaningless suddenly become glaringly obvious. Certain things people notice. Certain things they don't. Conversations that read one way the first time and completely differently afterwards. I immediately wanted to reread parts of the book just to see how much I'd missed.

Also — can we talk about how every single character annoyed me at some point? Not one of these people knows how to communicate like a normal human being. I loved it. They're messy, secretive, petty, unreliable, and impossible to trust. Exactly what I want in a mystery.

I don't think this tops The Killer Question for me — that one still has my whole heart — but Janice Hallett has officially convinced me that she could write 400 pages of emails between strangers and I'd still eat it up.

At this point I'm just accepting that I'm never going to outsmart her. And honestly? I'm okay with losing.

I gathered clues with great delight,
Convinced I'd solve the case that night.
She smiled, said “Nice try,” from page nineteen,
And proved I knew… absolutely nothing.
